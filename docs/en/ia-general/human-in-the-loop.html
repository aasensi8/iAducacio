02.1

PEOPLE · JUDGEMENT · ACCOUNTABILITY

Human in the loop

People stay at the centre of AI-supported work. AI can assist the process; human judgement and decisions remain with people.

Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) describes an approach in which people meaningfully participate in work with an AI system: they guide the task, interpret outputs, can correct them, and retain real decision-making power. The European Commission lists human agency and oversight among the requirements for trustworthy AI and describes different degrees of human control depending on the system and context. [35]

Human agency and oversight

In education, this means learners and teachers retain purpose, reasoning and responsibility. AI may help explore ideas, draft materials or offer hints; people need to understand, check and decide what to do with its suggestions. UNESCO recommends a human-centred approach and pedagogically appropriate interaction that preserves human agency. [36]

What does this mean in the classroom?

In a science task, a learner might ask AI for a hint about a step, check it against class materials, then explain why they use or reject it. AI supports the work; the learner keeps thinking and learning.

Legal precision. Article 14 of the EU AI Act sets human-oversight requirements for high-risk AI systems, with measures proportionate to risk, autonomy and context. This should not be read as a general legal duty to manually review every everyday chatbot response; HITL is also presented here as a pedagogical and accountability principle. [37]
