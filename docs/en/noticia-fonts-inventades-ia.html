When AI invents sources: why do we need to verify them?

31 October 2026 · iAducació

A bibliography can look flawless and still contain references that do not exist. Two recent reports show why we need to check sources generated or selected with artificial intelligence.

An academic reference promises that a source exists and can help readers check a claim. Yet generative AI can produce citations with convincing authors, titles and journals that cannot be found.

Suspicious references in scientific papers

An audit published in Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences (PNAS) examined 500,000 papers in physics, chemistry, materials science, engineering and computer science, published from 2023 to March 2026. It identified 1,135 suspicious references across 619 papers. Two experts confirmed that 85.8% of flagged references in a sample were fabricated.

The rate rose from 6.3 per 10,000 papers in 2023 to 15.4 in 2025 and 23.0 in the first quarter of 2026. The audit flags citations it could not locate; by itself, it does not prove that every false reference was generated by AI.

Read the Phys.org report · Read the PNAS study.

An experiment with ChatGPT, Gemini and an AI research system

In an experiment reported by IEEE Spectrum, journalist Benjamin Skuse used ChatGPT and Gemini to create a fictional paper about optical solitons: research that had never been done. IEEE Access integrity checks flagged its fabricated references.

A second test with FARS produced real references, but relied too heavily on preprints that had not yet undergone peer review. Checking that a reference exists is essential; we also need to assess its quality and relevance.

Read the IEEE Spectrum feature.

What can we learn in the classroom?

The Generalitat de Catalunya's AI in Education portal lists incorrect academic sources as one possible feature of AI-generated texts.

  • Search the exact title and check that the paper exists.
  • Verify the authors, journal, date and DOI.
  • Read the source and confirm it supports the claim.
  • Distinguish a peer-reviewed paper from a preprint.

See the Generalitat's indicator on AI-generated texts.

A classroom activity

Ask students to verify a reference suggested by AI: does it exist, who published it, and does it really support the claim?

AI can help us find and organise information, but it cannot replace verification. Checking sources is also a way to teach critical thinking and AI literacy.
