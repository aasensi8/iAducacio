The AI traffic light: five levels of permitted classroom use

17 November 2026 · iAducació

The same tool can support learning or replace the thinking we intend to assess. The INTEF guide proposes five traffic-light levels to clarify which forms of AI assistance are allowed before an assignment begins.

The five levels

1. Red · No AI

Complete the task independently, without AI assistance, so individual reasoning and skills remain visible.

2. Light amber · Ideas and structure

Use AI to brainstorm, explore approaches or outline a response; the finished work must be the learner’s own.

3. Amber · Editing and refinement

AI may improve clarity, wording or grammar in original work without adding substantially new content.

4. Light green · Specific tasks

AI may carry out specific tasks, but learners must critically check the results and disclose its use.

5. Green · AI as co-pilot

AI may support the entire creative process for complex outputs, while learners remain responsible for directing and checking the work.

What is it for?

The colour is not a grade or a rating of a learner: it defines the permitted level of assistance. This can vary with the learning objective. Verification, privacy protection and platform rules apply at every level.

An example from secondary-school science

In a density lesson, red might apply to individual mass-and-volume calculations; light amber to planning a report; amber to polishing the wording; light green to plotting non-personal data; and green to producing a teacher-supervised explanatory resource. These are examples devised by iAducació, not requirements set by INTEF.

Applying the model thoughtfully

Explain the permitted level before the task, identify what learners must do independently, gather evidence of learning and ask them to declare their actual AI use. The traffic light is pedagogical guidance, not a legal requirement or blanket permission for minors to use any AI service.

The key question

What should learners think through themselves, and which assistance supports rather than replaces that thinking?
