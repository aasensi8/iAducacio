New guidance on using AI in schools: what does it offer?

20 October 2026 · Writing and Education

How can we take advantage of artificial intelligence without delegating the teaching criterion or risking student data? The Department of Education and Vocational Training offers a framework to make these decisions in the center and classroom.

The Lesser Man Orientations for the use of artificial intelligence in educational centers, with first edition September 2026, update the 2024 guide and collect 45 orientations and annexes also addressed to students and families. It is an institutional orientation document: the pedagogical recommendations should be distinguished from the legal obligations that the text remembers. Credits, p. 2 · Guide update, p. 9.

Five ideas to bring to the cloister

1. First, descriptive decisions

The teacher defines the objectives, knowledge, evaluation criteria and supports. AI can help make materials, but it does not have to decide what we want students to learn. The document also proposes to declare the use of AI, both by faculty and students. Orientations, pp. 32–33.

2. Literacy is not just learning to do prompts

The progression combines instrumental mastery of the teacher, didactic use, competent use of students and use of AI to learn. For children under 14 years, the autonomous use of generalist generative AI is discouraged and literacy activities are proposed by the teacher. Always check the tool and center conditions. Progression, pp. 27–28 · Under 14 years, p. 25.

3. Protect data before sharing them

You must avoid entering personal data of own or third parties. In the analysis of data center, aggregate information and prevention of identification of people is prioritized. Family authorizations must be handled according to the context and conditions of the service: they do not replace the other guarantees. Personal data, p. 13 · Authorizations and data analysis, pp. 20–22.

4. Evaluate the process and maintain the teaching trial

It is recommended to collect drafts, oral explanations, reviews and evidence of the process. The teacher makes the assessment first; the subsequent support of AI must be limited. The document discourages the evaluations. AI detectors, because of the errors and risks they carry, and stresses learning. Evaluation, pp. 37–40.

5. A centre, shared and revisable agreement

The incorporation of AI requires common criteria, training of the cloister and information to familiesThe framework of responsible use must specify tools, responsibilities and monitoring mechanisms; it is not only an individual choice of each teacher. Centre Organization, pp. 18–23 · Families, pp. 45–46.

A proposal to start

Proposal of iAducació: devote a department or teaching team meeting to revise a specific activity. What will the teacher decide? What data will be used? How will learning become visible? What AI intervention will be declared?
